We are delighted to share that **Prof. I-Yun Lisa Hsieh** has received the **Outstanding Teaching Award (優良教學獎)** from National Taiwan University — the university's annual recognition of teaching excellence among its faculty.

### Teaching as Part of the Mission

At the E3 group, education is not a side activity of research — it is how the energy transition actually gets staffed. Prof. Hsieh teaches across the Department of Civil Engineering and her joint appointment in Chemical Engineering, bringing the lab's research themes — energy systems, low-carbon transportation, and environmental policy — into the classroom with the same integrated, quantitative approach the group applies in its research.

### An Education Thread That Runs Through the Lab

The award sits within a broader commitment to energy education that spans well beyond NTU's campus:

- A Ministry of Education project developing **cross-disciplinary curriculum modules for sustainable energy**, followed by the ongoing **Green Engineering Interdisciplinary Micro-Program** connecting engineering students with industry;
- Outreach to the next generation, from the [Energy Thinking Bootcamp](/news/2026-wego-bootcamp/) to the [Student Research Showcase](/news/2026-wego-research-showcase/) developed with Wego High School;
- Research mentoring that shows in the lab's student honors — from an [NSTC undergraduate research grant](/news/2026-nstc-undergraduate-grant/) to graduate awards such as the [CAEE outstanding thesis](/news/2025-caee-outstanding-thesis/) and a stream of best-paper and poster prizes at domestic and international conferences.

Recognitions like this one reflect what students already tell us: rigorous, systems-level thinking about energy and the environment can be taught — and taught well.
