**[Prof. I-Yun Lisa Hsieh](/members/iyunlisahsieh/)** brought the lab's research to a broad public audience with a column in **The News Lens (關鍵評論網)**, one of Taiwan's leading digital media outlets: [*「2030年電動車全面替代燃油車願景中，退役電池和車聯網都是「能源永續」的一環」*](https://www.thenewslens.com/article/204563) — arguing that in the vision of fully replacing fuel vehicles by 2030, retired batteries and connected vehicles are both pieces of the energy-sustainability puzzle.

### The Argument

Published in July 2024, the column makes two connected cases:

- **Give batteries a second life before recycling them.** When an EV or e-scooter battery retires from the road, it typically still holds substantial capacity. Cascading these packs into stationary storage (梯次利用) — for homes, buildings, and industrial sites — extends their useful life, supports the grid during peak demand, and keeps households powered through outages, all before the materials head to recycling.
- **Treat connected vehicles as grid assets.** Through Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) technology, EV fleets can charge when demand is low and discharge support when the grid is stressed — turning millions of parked vehicles into a distributed energy resource rather than a pure load.

The column surveys international momentum — Norway's incentive-driven adoption, the EV100 corporate fleet-electrification initiative — and argues Taiwan should pair progressive subsidies with clear mandates while building the second-life ecosystem ahead of the retirement wave.

### The Research Behind the Op-Ed

These are the questions the E3 group works on daily: the [smart battery-swap optimization project](/projects/2024-smart-battery-swap-v2b/) and our [V2G research in e-scooter battery-swapping ecosystems](/news/2025-asia-research-news-v2g/) put numbers behind exactly the vision the column sketches for the public.
