## 1. Executive summary

The site has steady but narrow visibility on Google. Across the year it earned **806 clicks from 44,983 impressions** (1.79% CTR, average position 8.73), but those headline numbers are heavily distorted: a single ambiguous query — bare **"e3"** — generated **77% of all impressions and only 1.7% of clicks**, almost certainly from people searching for the E3 video-game expo rather than this research center.

Stripping out that noise, the real picture is:

| Segment | Clicks | Impressions | CTR | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Brand (`e3 center`, `e3 group`, `e3 lab`, …) | **200** | 897 | **22.3%** | Strong brand recognition. People who know the lab find it. |
| Names (PI + students) | 68 | 1,803 | 3.8% | Decent but room to improve titles/snippets. |
| Generic "e3" variants | 14 | 34,604 | 0.04% | Noise. Disregard for trend analysis. |
| Other (non-brand, non-name) | 1 | 363 | 0.28% | **Effectively zero topical/research traffic.** |

The structural takeaways:

1. **Discovery is brand-only.** The site ranks for *who you are*, not *what you do*. No research-topic queries (energy, transportation, sustainability, policy) appear in the top 270 queries.
2. **Indexing breadth is the single biggest issue.** Only **9 URLs** received any impressions all year — the homepage absorbed >97% of them. 41 member entries exist; only 1 member page surfaces meaningfully in Search.
3. **Canonical / duplicate-URL problems** exist for member subpages (with vs. without trailing slash).
4. **Brand CTR is excellent** when intent matches; the work to do is widening the funnel above brand search.

### 5.1 Indexing gap

The site has **41 member entries** across `members.json` (5 sections) and **26 per-member JSON files**, plus a publications page, a research page, a group-life page, a videos page, and multiple news items. Of all of these, only **3 distinct member pages** and **1 listing page (`/news/`)** ever showed up in Search this year.

This is the single highest-leverage problem in the report. Most of the site is invisible to Google.

Likely causes to investigate:
- Sitemap coverage — verify `docs/sitemap.xml` includes all member pages, news items, and topic pages.
- `robots.txt` rules.
- Internal linking — if member pages aren't linked from the homepage with crawlable anchors, discovery is slow.
- Recency — many member subpages are recent additions (the current branch `seo-search-ranking` is actively enriching them).
- `noindex` / canonical headers (less likely here, but worth checking).

### 5.2 Trailing-slash duplicates

Two members appear under both URL forms:

| URL form | Impressions |
|---|---:|
| `/members/yuanhsichien` (no slash) | 11 |
| `/members/yuanhsichien/` (with slash) | 7 |
| `/members/yutinghuang` (no slash) | 3 |
| `/members/yutinghuang/` (with slash) | 15 |

Google is treating these as separate URLs and splitting ranking signals. Pick one canonical form (the project convention appears to be trailing-slash directory pages) and 301-redirect the other, or set `<link rel="canonical">` consistently.

### 5.3 The orphan URL

`/Solar-PV-on-Bus-Shelter/` got 6 impressions at position 7.8 but is not a current site URL pattern (members live at `/members/...`, news at `/news/...`). Worth checking whether this is a stale URL Google still has cached, a legacy redirect, or a one-off page.

### 5.4 Image asset ranking

`/assets/images/e3-logo-text.svg` showing in Web results (4 impressions, pos 35) is harmless but suggests the SVG is being treated as a standalone document. Adding a `Content-Disposition` or ensuring it's only ever embedded is a minor cleanup, not urgent.

## 9. Recommendations

Prioritized by leverage (impact × ease):

### Tier 1 — biggest impact, mostly already in scope on the `seo-search-ranking` branch

1. **Fix indexing breadth.** Only 9 URLs got impressions this year out of dozens that exist. Concrete steps:
    - Verify the generated `docs/sitemap.xml` lists every member page, news item, and topic page; check Search Console "Pages" report (Indexing → Pages) for the difference between *Discovered* and *Indexed*.
    - In Search Console, use **URL Inspection → Request Indexing** for the top 10 most important pages (PI profile, top members, `/publications/`, `/research/`, `/news/`, recent news items).
    - Audit internal linking: ensure each member is linked from `/members/` (and ideally cross-linked from publications/news), with crawlable `<a href>` and not JS-only navigation.
    - Confirm `robots.txt` and meta-robots on subpages are not inadvertently blocking.

2. **Resolve trailing-slash duplicates.** `yuanhsichien` and `yutinghuang` are indexed under both URL forms. Pick one canonical form repo-wide and either 301-redirect or set `<link rel="canonical">` on the loser. (See `/members/...` URL convention in `pages.json`.)

3. **Rewrite `<title>` and meta description on the homepage and PI profile** to win the queries you already rank for but don't click on:
    - **`i yun`** (pos 3.4, 154 impr, 0 clicks) and **`i-yun lisa hsieh`** (pos 3.5, 9.8% CTR — could be 20%+) — make the PI's name and lab unmistakable in the SERP snippet.
    - **`謝依芸`** and **`謝依芸 台大`** — include the Chinese name AND "台大 / NTU / 國立臺灣大學" in the homepage and `/members/iyunlisahsieh/` titles.
    - **`caece.net`** — domain-typed searches mean users are looking for *this site* but the snippet isn't reassuring. Homepage title should clearly read as "E3 Center · National Taiwan University" or similar.

### Tier 2 — medium impact, modest effort

4. **Build topical content to escape brand-only ranking.** Currently zero non-brand topical queries appear in the top 270. Consider:
    - Short topic landing pages or research-area summaries (e.g. `/research/transportation-electrification/`, `/research/solar-pv/`) with descriptive H1s and bodies. The orphan `/Solar-PV-on-Bus-Shelter/` impression hints there's already external interest in this topic.
    - News items currently exist as separate URLs but only `/news/` (the listing) ranks — individual stories should each surface for topical queries given enough body text and good titles.

5. **Push `e3 group` from pos 14.8 → top 10.** The query already converts at 17.9% CTR; rank improvement compounds. Make sure the homepage and `/members/` page both feature "E3 Group" in headings.

6. **Improve `/news/` ranking for `e3 news`** (currently pos 62.8). The page exists; clearly Google doesn't see it as the canonical answer for the query. Title/H1 alignment + a few inbound internal links should fix this.

### Tier 3 — nice-to-have, longer horizon

7. **Bilingual / hreflang setup.** International traffic (US, Japan, SG, IN, DE, NL) has high CTR but small volume. Translated results show up in GSC but never click. A clean EN/中 split with `hreflang` annotations would let the international audience self-select and convert better.

8. **Mentally exclude bare "e3"** from headline metrics. When the team reviews GSC monthly, look at *non-brand impressions* and *non-"e3"* CTR separately; otherwise the gaming-expo noise will keep distorting the signal. (In Search Console, add a query filter: `Query does not contain "e3 expo"` won't help, but `Query is not "e3"` will.)

9. **Submit to Google Scholar / structured citations.** Publication pages have JSON-LD now; ensure each publication's authors link to the correct member profile (per `excel_to_content.py` autolink logic) — this both helps the indexing problem and starts to build topical authority for each member's research area.

10. **Re-export this report quarterly** and watch:
    - Total non-brand impressions (the leading indicator for topical SEO).
    - Number of distinct URLs receiving impressions (the leading indicator for indexing breadth).
    - Average position on `e3 group`, `謝依芸`, and `i yun` (the easiest wins on the table today).

## 10. Reading these numbers in the future

A few interpretive rules so this report's caveats survive past the immediate context:

- **CTR aggregates lie when one query dominates.** The bare "e3" query inflated impressions and crushed average CTR. Always check the query mix before reading aggregate CTR.
- **Average position is impression-weighted in GSC.** A site with 90% of impressions from one query at pos 8 will report "average position 8" even if every other query ranks at pos 3.
- **Search Console anonymizes rare queries.** Sum of per-query clicks/impr will be lower than the Chart-sheet totals; the gap is the "anonymous" tail.
- **Position is averaged across all impressions.** A query showing pos 8.6 may rank pos 1 for some users and pos 30 for others (different geographies/devices/personalization). Big numbers (pos 40+, pos 60+) usually mean "rarely ever ranks, but did once").
- **Date in this export = date Google logged the impression**, not date of any change on the site. Allow 1–4 weeks for site changes to show up in search behavior.
